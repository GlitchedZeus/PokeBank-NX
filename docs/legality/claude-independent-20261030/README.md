## Milestone 1: the source-save slot settles the Day Care level/EXP exception

`Legality::Context::storage` (`SlotStorage`: `Unknown` by default, `PartyOrBox`, `DayCare`) is
passed to `GB::Entity1/2::storage`.

Evidence: a party or PC box slot is never out of step with its EXP.
- pokered `engine/pokemon/add_mon.asm` `_MoveMon` recomputes the level with
  `CalcLevelFromExperience` on every return to the party (box and Day Care).
- pokecrystal `engine/pokemon/move_mon.asm` `SendGetMonIntoFromBox` calls `CalcLevel`, and
  `RetrieveBreedmon` sets the level from EXP, then resets EXP to that level's total.
- Battle EXP, Rare Candy and deposits keep the two in step (`levelExpProblem` comment).

What changes:
- `PartyOrBox`: a stale level and EXP above the level-100 total (within the Day Care step
  counter's `$50FFFF` reach) become definitive Invalid.
- `DayCare`: both are that slot's own behaviour, so nothing is reported.
- Level ahead of EXP and EXP past `$50FFFF` stay Invalid in every slot.
- `Unknown` keeps the existing Warning, so no current caller changes verdict.

Measured on the fresh corpora above, as if every record came from a party/box slot:

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PK1 | 277 | 187 | 90 |
| PK2 | 96 | 31 | 65 |

The stricter rows are stale levels that PKHeX derives away, because it computes the level
from EXP and never compares the two.

## Milestone 2: party max HP (beyond PKHeX)

PKHeX 26.10.18 does not compare stored GB party stats: it accepted 401/401 PK1 and 401/401 PK2
records whose max HP was nudged by one.

Evidence:
- The games compute max HP only from the record's own level, HP DV and the stat experience of
  that moment (pokered `home/move_mon.asm` `CalcStat`, pokecrystal `CalcMonStatC` +
  `GetSquareRoot`).
- They recompute it on every level-up, Rare Candy, evolution and return to the party.
- A catch reloads it fresh from the original DVs, even after Transform (pokered `ItemUseBall`
  → `LoadEnemyMonData`, pokecrystal `PokeBallEffect` → `LoadEnemyMon`).
- Battle never modifies max HP, and stat experience never decreases.

So the stored value is the formula at the current level for some HP stat experience between
0 and the stored one. (Milestone 3 extends this to the other stats.)

What the rule does:
- `partyMaxHpProblem` in `GBLegality.cpp` (now `partyStatProblems`) makes a mismatch definitive.
- The base HP came from `include/Legality/GBBaseHP.inc`, generated by `tools/gen_gb_base_hp.py`
  from pokecrystal and cross-checked against pokered for 1–151 (both replaced in Milestone 3 by
  `GBBaseStats.inc` / `tools/gen_gb_base_stats.py`). CI re-checks it in
  `legality-gb-pkhex-diff.yml`.
- `Legality.cpp` applies the rule only when the record's own `maxHP` and level equal the
  stored party-struct bytes they were parsed from (`rawBody`, 44/48 bytes).
- A stored 0 is skipped. It means a box struct in party layout, as in several PKHeX public
  test files; a real party struct can't hold it, since the minimum is level + 10.

Tests:
- `test_gb_legality_structural`: a 251-species × 6-level × 4-DV × 5-stat-experience × 11-value
  grid against the games' formula, run over every stat experience value up to the stored one
  (876,648 checks). Base HP there comes from the PKSM port, so it is independent of the
  pret-generated table.
- The same test runs real Red saves and native PK2 party records through `Legality::analyze`.
- The 30 PKHeX public PK1/PK2 files produce no max-HP finding.
- The differential corpora now end with `defect-party-max-hp` / `defect2-party-max-hp`
  records (count/200, appended after the seeded stream so earlier ids are unchanged). In the
  12,000-record run the first 12,000 verdicts were byte-identical, and all 60 PK1 and 59 PK2
  defects are classified `deliberate: party-max-hp` by a predicate that recomputes
  reachability from PKHeX's own `personal_c` base HP. The 60th PK2 record was already
  PKHeX-Invalid.

Found while cross-checking: the PKSM-derived `src/Integration/Gen2/Gen2PersonalData.cpp`
has wrong base HP for Granbull (99, should be 90) and Magby (74, should be 45). pret and PKHeX
agree on 90/45, and every other Gen II base stat matches pret. That table belongs to the
editor/app (Main Development) and was not modified. The test encodes the two errata
explicitly.

## Milestone 3: the other party stats (beyond PKHeX)

Milestone 2 left Attack, Defense, Speed and Special alone on the theory that battle effects
or the Time Capsule might reach them. The disassemblies rule both out:

- Battle stages, burn and paralysis act on the battle copies (`wBattleMon*` / `wEnemyMon*`).
  A catch reloads the wild Pokemon's stats from its species, level and original DVs before it
  is stored (pokecrystal `PokeBallEffect` `.caught` → `LoadEnemyMon`, which also covers the
  Transform case; pokered `ItemUseBall` → `LoadEnemyMonData`).
- The Time Capsule copies max HP, Attack, Defense and Speed, whose bases are identical in both
  generations (checked for 1–151 by the generator), and recomputes the Special stats from the
  receiving generation's bases with the current stat experience: Gen I → II computes Special
  Attack and Special Defense from the Gen II bases (`Link_ConvertPartyStruct1to2`), Gen II → I
  computes Special from `KantoMonSpecials` (`.ConvertPartyStruct2to1`), both with
  `CalcMonStatC` and `b = TRUE`.
- Vitamins, Rare Candy, level-up, evolution and every return to the party recompute all stats
  from the stat experience of that moment (pokecrystal `CalcMonStats`, pokered `CalcStats`).
- The non-HP formula is `((base + DV) * 2 + b / 4) * level / 100 + 5`, capped at 999, in both
  games; Gen II's Special Attack and Special Defense both use the Special DV and Special stat
  experience.

So every stored stat is the formula at the current level for some stat experience between 0
and the stored one. `partyStatProblems` checks each stat on its own (the games set all of them
in one recalculation, so the joint condition is stronger; checking them separately is weaker
but never stricter). A stored max HP of 0 still skips the whole snapshot.

Data: `include/Legality/GBBaseStats.inc`, generated by `tools/gen_gb_base_stats.py` from
pokecrystal (HP, Attack, Defense, Speed, Special Attack, Special Defense) and pokered (Gen I
Special), which must agree on HP/Attack/Defense/Speed for 1–151; with `--pokeyellow`, Yellow's
table must equal Red/Blue's. CI runs it with all three.

Tests:
- `test_gb_legality_structural`: a grid over 251 species × 4 levels × 3 DV sets × 5 stats ×
  3 stat-experience values × 9 stored values against the games' formula and every stat
  experience up to the stored one, for PK2 and (Attack, Defense, Speed) PK1, checking that only
  the stat under test is reported. Bases come from the PKSM port; it agrees with pret on every
  non-HP Gen II base. Psyduck checks the Gen I Special (50) against the Gen II Special Attack
  (65), a halved Attack is rejected, and real Red saves and native PK2 party records run through
  `Legality::analyze` with each stat wrong. 3,869,783 checks in total.
- Fixtures: the Red save's placeholder stats (230, 229, 228, 227) and the Gen I fixture's
  (8, 7, 9, 7) now carry the formula values.
- Differential, seed 20261031, 12,000 records per format, PKHeX 26.10.18: the corpora now also
  end with `defect-party-stat` / `defect2-party-stat` records, one non-HP stat nudged by one.
  The first 12,000 verdicts are byte-identical to the Milestone 2 run. PK1: 120 records
  `deliberate: party-stats` (60 max HP + 60 other stats), 0 differing. PK2: 117 deliberate
  (59 + 58; three were already PKHeX-Invalid), and the same two pre-existing differing records
  as before (#9716, the hand-verified Bulbasaur-father ancestry, and #11060, an unauthenticated
  gift ancestry kept Incomplete). Production parity passes for both formats.

## Milestone 4: current HP never above max HP (beyond PKHeX)

The games cap battle damage and healing at the max HP. A level-up, Rare Candy or evolution
adds the max-HP gain to the current HP (pokered `experience.asm`, `evos_moves.asm` and `item_effects.asm` around
`CalcStats`; pokecrystal `evolve.asm`, `battle/core.asm` and `item_effects.asm` around `CalcMonStats`). Every
other recalculation (a return to the party, vitamins, the Time Capsule) keeps the current HP
while max HP stays or grows, because EXP and stat experience only grow. A catch saves and
restores the wild Pokemon's current HP around `LoadEnemyMon`.

- Party structs (PK1 and PK2): current HP at most the stored max HP, when the stored bytes are
  the ones the record was parsed from (`partyStats`).
- Gen I box structs keep the current HP they were deposited with, and pokered `_MoveMon`
  writes the party level into the box level byte on deposit. So a box record's current HP is
  at most the formula's max HP at its stored level with its own HP stat experience. A Gen II
  box struct has no current HP.
- `currentHpProblem` in `GBLegality.cpp`; `Entity1/2::hpKnown` / `currentHP` are set by
  `Legality.cpp` from the raw struct bytes only (`rawBody`, 44/33 bytes for PK1, 48 for PK2).

Tests: an engine table of party current HP values, a PK1 box grid (151 species × 4 levels ×
3 DV sets × 3 stat-experience values × 5 values, PKSM bases) and real Red saves (party
200/0/201 of 200; a box Bulbasaur with 200/201 HP and no HP stat experience, 263/264 with
65535). 3,896,994 structural checks in total.

Differential, same seed: the corpora end with `defect-current-hp` / `defect2-current-hp`
(current HP one above max). PKHeX 26.10.18 accepts all 60 + 60; they are classified with the
`party-stats` predicate (current HP above the stored max HP), and the first 12,000 verdicts
are byte-identical. PK1: 0 differing. PK2: the same two pre-existing records. Production
parity passes for both formats.

## Milestone 5: status byte and the Gen I box-level byte (beyond PKHeX)

Status condition byte (PK1 party and box structs offset 0x04, PK2 party struct offset 32):
0, a sleep counter 1–7, or exactly one of PSN (0x08), BRN (0x10), FRZ (0x20), PAR (0x40).
Every writer was audited in pokered, pokeyellow, pokecrystal and pokegold:
- Whole-value stores: sleep counters (`SleepEffect`, `BattleCommand_SleepTarget`, the
  disobedient nap, Rest), the side-effect burn/freeze/paralysis (`ld a, 1 << X`), cures (0).
- Bit sets only after the byte was checked to be 0: pokered `effects.asm` `PoisonEffect`,
  `move_effects/paralyze.asm`; pokecrystal/pokegold `effect_commands.asm` `PoisonOpponent`
  (both callers check `and a / jr nz`), `BurnTarget`, `FreezeTarget`, `ParalyzeTarget`,
  `BattleCommand_Paralyze`.
- Party/box structs copy the battle byte (`ReadPlayerMonCurHPAndStatus`,
  `UpdateBattleMonInParty`, catches, deposits). Bit 7 has no writer.

A fainted Pokemon may keep its status: a skeptic review found that recoil or Destiny Bond can
knock out both sides and save HP 0 with PSN/BRN/PAR (pokered `HandleEnemyMonFainted` skips
`RemoveFaintedPlayerMon` in that case; pokecrystal `UpdateBattleMonInParty` on the wild
path), so "fainted means no status" is deliberately **not** a rule. A PK2 egg must have status
0: eggs are made with 0 (Day Care `ByteFill`, `GiveEgg` → `TryAddMonToParty`, the Odd Egg
table) and never battle.

Gen I party struct box-level byte (0x03): `_AddPartyMon` writes 0 for catches, gifts and
trades into the party; `_MoveMon` writes the party level on PARTY_TO_BOX/PARTY_TO_DAYCARE and
leaves it on withdrawal; level-up, Rare Candy and evolution write only the party level; the
battle struct copies it out and back unchanged (Transform skips it); the Time Capsule writes 0
(`.ConvertPartyStruct2to1`). So it is at most the party level. Assumption, stated: Pokemon
Stadium (not disassembled) moves Pokemon by copying, and cannot lower a level.

PKHeX 26.10.18 checks neither (no PK1/PK2 status or box-level verifier). Differential, same
seed: appended controls with a single condition (20 PK1, 30 PK2) are legal in both; 20 PK1 and
30 PK2 two-condition/bit-7 bytes and 20 PK1 box-level bytes above the level are classified
`deliberate: status-byte` / `box-level`. First 12,000 verdicts byte-identical; production
parity passes. Structural tests: every status value for PK1, fainted PK1, PK2 and PK2 eggs,
every box-level value, and Red save party and box records (3,899,104 checks).
